Thobii — a product case study

An open-loop RFID fuel wallet for Saudi Arabia · concept → live demo

The Problem

Saudi fuel stations are full-service by regulation — an attendant pumps every fill. Payment is still the slowest, most error-prone step: cash handling, card terminals at the pump lane, wrong fuel grades going into tanks, and fleets that can't control what their drivers spend. Aldrees proved the fix works: its RFID system (WAIE) runs on 629 stations and 623,000+ vehicles — tap, fuel, drive off.

The Insight

WAIE isn't a payments business — it's a lock-in machine. ~97% of Aldrees revenue is fuel sales; WAIE exists to keep fleets fueling only at Aldrees. It's a closed loop: one brand's tag, one brand's stations.

That's the structural gap. Aldrees can't open the loop without breaking its own lock-in. A neutral wallet that works across every station brand is a product the incumbent is structurally unable to copy.

The Wedge — an open-loop wallet

One wallet, one tag, any station brand. Thobii monetizes like a payments company, not a fuel retailer: a per-fill merchant fee charged to stations for the volume Thobii routes to them, plus fleet controls as the B2B hook.

Average fill (40 L Gasoline 95 @ SAR 2.33)≈ SAR 93
Per-fill fee to station (illustrative ~1%)≈ SAR 0.90
Fleet vehicle × 8 fills / month≈ SAR 7.20 / vehicle / mo
At 100k vehicles (16% of WAIE's base)≈ SAR 8.6M / year

Honest tension: fuel retail margins are thin, so a fee only clears if it pays for itself — incremental routed volume, less cash handling, less fraud. That's why the go-to-market starts with challenger station brands hungry for fleet volume, not the leader. Float on prepaid balances and fleet SaaS are secondary lines.

Key Product Decisions
Hold → capture, not one charge. The pump can't know the final amount before fueling, so authorize places a hold for the maximum affordable volume and settle captures the metered amount. Same model card networks use at pumps. Tradeoff: expiry and release logic to build and test.
Research corrected the model. The first version let the user "choose litres." Studying WAIE showed the truth: litres come from the pump's flow meter, and the attendant pumps — the driver never enters a number. The UI was relabelled to match reality.
Misfuel prevention at authorize. Each vehicle is locked to a grade; the wrong nozzle is refused before a drop flows — mirrors WAIE's nozzle-reader verification.
Weekly litre caps. Building limits revealed the real buyer: the fleet manager, not the driver. Caps, velocity limits, and one-active-fill-per-vehicle are the B2B product.
Money that can't lie. Append-only double-sided ledger, integer halalas (no floats), idempotency keys on every money endpoint, reconciliation reports. 99 automated tests against a real Postgres.
Try-it distribution. The live demo runs a full in-browser mock — anyone can authorize, fill, and read a receipt without signing up.
What's Real, What's Not

Genuinely built and tested: the money core — append-only ledger, hold → capture authorization, idempotency keys on every money endpoint, integer money math (no floats), wallet locking under concurrency, velocity and volume limits, daily reconciliation — verified by 99 automated tests against a real PostgreSQL.

Deliberately out of scope at this stage: live payment rails (top-ups are simulated), KYC/AML, user authentication, and physical pump integration. Each is scoped, not forgotten — rails via a mada/PSP gateway, KYC through a licensed partner, and the flow-meter settle spec sits on the roadmap.

Knowing which half is which is the credibility test for any fintech demo.

Regulation

A stored-value wallet in Saudi Arabia is regulated e-money. Path: launch inside the SAMA Regulatory Sandbox or ride on a licensed EMI partner, with mada / Apple Pay rails for top-ups — licensing is a launch dependency, not an afterthought.

Success Metrics

North star: monthly settled fills per active wallet — it proves habit, routed volume, and fee revenue in one number.

Guardrails: authorization failure rate · reversal rate · top-up → first-fill conversion · dormant balance share (float that signals churn, not profit).

What's Next

1. Pilot with one challenger station network (prove open loop) · 2. Fleet dashboard — multi-vehicle caps, consumption reports, VAT invoices · 3. SAMA sandbox application · 4. Pump integration spec: flow-meter settle over webhook, replacing the simulator.

Built end-to-end — market research, product decisions, working ledger backend, and this UI — using AI-assisted development, shipped as a live demo. The simulator upstairs is the product; this page is the reasoning.
